How it works

Three seconds from wondering to knowing

No star charts to learn, no menus to dig through. Raise your phone and the sky explains itself.

STEP 01

Raise your phone

The camera opens instantly. GPS, gyroscope and compass lock your exact position and where you're aiming — no calibration ceremony.

STEP 02

Everything gets a name

Live labels pin themselves to objects in the frame — stars, constellations, planets, the Moon, aircraft and satellites, each with a one-line story.

STEP 03

Tap to go deeper

Open a full card with rise and set times, distance, brightness, orbital path and the best night to look again. Save it to your sky journal.

Questions

Good to know

The star, constellation, planet and Moon catalogue lives on your device, so the core AR experience works with no signal at all. Live aircraft, satellite tracking and ISS pass updates need a connection.

Yes — you can point at the daytime sky and see the Sun, the Moon, aircraft and the ISS overlaid through the screen. Stars and planets are shown on the map layer, since they're simply not bright enough to see by eye in daylight.

SkyFinder fuses GPS, gyroscope, accelerometer and magnetometer data, then cross-checks against the star field it sees to correct drift. Typical accuracy is under 0.2 degrees. If your phone's compass needs a nudge, the app walks you through a two-second figure-eight calibration.

iPhone XS and newer running iOS 16+, and Android 10+ devices with ARCore support. Older phones can still use the sky map and Tonight view without the camera overlay.

No. Frames are processed entirely on-device and discarded immediately — we never store or transmit your camera feed. Location is used only to compute your sky and is never sold or shared.

SkyFinder is built around a near-black interface and a low-power sensor mode. A typical half-hour session uses a few percent of battery. There's also a Battery Saver mode that drops to a 5 fps overlay for long sessions.
